The challenge

Decision points keep coming for years. The evidence behind them rarely keeps up.

Programs, policies and products run for years, and organizations commit resources and draw on data all the way through. But evidence is gathered at one moment and ages quickly. Using it well depends on teams knowing what to draw on, when, and when to commission something new.

Where HIE comes inBring that evidence together into a usable, living tool for resource allocation and decision-making, from first design through scale.
  1. 01
    Different moments need different evidence

    Early strategy needs the broader picture; design needs niche segments; scale-up needs recent data from the places you are expanding into.

  2. 02
    Research is fragmented and goes stale

    Surveys, monitoring data, evaluations and local knowledge sit across programs and partners, and are rarely brought together at the point of decision.

  3. 03
    Assumptions go untested, and new data comes late

    Teams move forward without knowing which assumptions carry the greatest risk, or commission research after the decision it should have informed.

    Know the limits

    Where HIE is not the right tool today

    HIE adds the most value when sharpening hypotheses, comparing design and delivery options, surfacing likely friction, and deciding where real-world research is worth commissioning. Field evidence and human judgment remain essential for:

    1. Determining whether an intervention worksSimulations give directional signals; causal effectiveness needs real-world testing.
    2. Understanding lived experience in fullVirtual personas cannot replace direct engagement with people and communities as contexts change.
    3. Confirming feasibility and actual behaviorField validation is still needed for implementation realities, uptake and sustained use.
    4. Exercising expert and contextual judgmentTeams and experts frame the questions, review assumptions and interpret results.
    5. Making high-stakes decisions on its ownHIE informs, but does not determine, funding, policy, program or individual decisions.

    From real-world evidence to dynamic virtual worlds

    How the Human Insights Engine turns evidence into bounded, context-aware, persistent in memory, and auditable virtual personas, and how it checks that what they tell us holds up.

    Real world evidence, simulated feedback, informed decisions.

    The Human Insights Engine (HIE) is an evidence-informed and AI-powered simulation tool that helps development organizations explore potential responses to a proposed policy, program, product, service or message before resources are committed. It turns relevant real-world evidence into directional hypotheses for a defined population, geography, behavioral question, and use case. It is not a replacement for real-world activities, but a conduit and complementary asset to problem-solve faster, more robustly, and with more resource-efficient approaches. It works very well with established real-world research, data collection, prototyping, pilots, and at-scale implementation practices.

    HIE does not write virtual personas as character descriptions or prompts. It converts real-world evidence into machine-readable behavioral agents, places them in structured social and research environments, and runs repeated simulations under explicit rules, constraints and confidence checks. The result is simulation that is bounded by evidence, persistent in memory over time and auditable.

    02How the engine was trained

    HIE began as a shell: machine learning code that defines how a virtual persona is constructed, with metrics across four categories: demographics, psychographics, behaviors and context. On its own, the shell knows the shape of a virtual person but nothing about specific characteristics that define it.

    We trained it on a wide range of real-world data, including human-centered design primary research, in-field observations, household and national surveys, census data, quantitative research studies, clinical and program data, longitudinal series data, and more. Large language models (LLMs) made the training fast and feasible, reading and structuring very different sources and mapping them into the shell's common schema. That structured evidence trained the machine learning code and let us find the right weights, turning a static shell into a dynamic machine learning schema that can generate new virtual personas whenever new data is loaded.

    Two principles that keep the engine honest

    Principle 1

    Trained only on inputs

    When we use a study, we train only on its original responses, never on the study's outputs or findings.

    Principle 2

    Simulated data never goes back in

    Nothing the engine generates is ever fed back into the platform as training data.

    Both guard against the same failure. Feeding in simulated data risks hallucination compounding over time. Feeding in a study's outputs risks the model, over time, referencing those answers instead of reasoning from what people actually said and did.

    It also means there is only one route to improving HIE: more detailed, more comprehensive, higher-quality input data. When a simulation needs to be more accurate, we improve what goes in, never by feeding back what comes out.

    Inputs are the past, a study's outputs are the present, and simulations are the future.Trained only on inputs, the engine has no sense of past, present or future. Reproducing a study that has already happened and anticipating one that has not are the same task, which is why HIE's validation results are a strong anchor for trusting its simulations.

    03How a virtual persona is built

    A virtual persona in HIE is a structured state model, not a character sketch. Each one is represented through four layers, which together decide what it can be, how it tends to behave, what can change, and where every part of it came from.

    A · Fixed core

    Who the virtual persona is

    The segment boundary it cannot casually drift outside.

    Geography, age band, household, livelihood, literacy, language, socioeconomic context, structural constraints

    B · Behavioral priors

    How it tends to behave

    Relatively stable tendencies, calibrated as ranges rather than single fixed numbers.

    Risk tolerance, present bias, agency, norms, stigma, preferences, trust tendencies, channel use, price sensitivity

    C · Dynamic state

    What can change

    States that evolve as it meets information, people or events, while the fixed core stays intact.

    Trust, relevance, emotion, intent, perceived risk, feasibility, social permission, salience

    D · Evidence and memory

    Where it came from

    Links behavior back to provenance and prior experience, so it is traceable rather than purely generative.

    Source IDs, sampling frame, recency, evidence limitations and anchors, prior interactions, observed outcomes, trust matrix

    From evidence to a calibrated agent

    Building a virtual persona follows five steps: retrieve evidence, map it to the virtual persona schema, calibrate attributes and behavioral ranges, instantiate stochastic latent variation, and validate constraints and provenance.

    Calibration is where the shell becomes dynamic, and it never involves feeding in outputs. Simulated behavior is compared with what was observed in the real world. Where accuracy falls short, the weights for new virtual personas are adjusted by feeding in more detailed, more comprehensive, higher-quality input data until simulations reach the accuracy needed. Once calibrated, the same machinery can take in new data and generate new virtual personas without being rebuilt.

    Stochastic Latent Seeding (SLS) is what turns one segment into many distinct virtual personas. The segment defines the behavioral envelope, and SLS decides where a particular virtual persona starts inside it. Two virtual personas from the same segment might begin with slightly different levels of trust, household pressure or risk tolerance, while both staying inside the calibrated evidence boundaries.

    Virtual persona schema = what this person can plausibly be. SLS = which plausible version is instantiated for this run.This is why HIE produces a distribution of plausible responses rather than one deterministic answer.

    What a virtual persona is, and is not, allowed to do

    1. Hard constraints do not drift.Core demographic and structural attributes stay fixed unless a simulation explicitly models their change.
    2. Variation stays inside calibrated boundaries.Stochasticity creates heterogeneity, not arbitrary behavior.
    3. Dynamic states change through events and interaction.Trust, intent, relevance, emotion and feasibility move as the scenario unfolds.
    4. Evidence gaps remain visible.Where evidence is thin, the system surfaces uncertainty rather than silently generalizing.
    5. State transitions are traceable.Changes in state, assumptions and evidence used in a run are logged.
    6. The model does not redefine the virtual persona.The underlying language model is frozen and works inside boundaries set by the engine.

    04From virtual personas to virtual worlds

    Individual virtual personas become useful when they are placed in context. HIE layers four structures on top of them.

    The interaction graph governs how virtual personas affect each other. HIE does not simply concatenate responses. Each exchange is interpreted through relationships, trust, authority, social norms, influence and previous interactions, and can shift dynamic states such as trust or social permission.

    Units and cohorts assemble virtual personas into households, panels, networks and communities against a sampling frame, with weights so the cohort represents a larger real population. Aggregation does not erase individual state.

    The instrument runner applies qualitative, quantitative and specialised research methods across the cohort. A virtual persona can move along a behavioral pathway, from exposure and attention through comprehension, credibility, personal relevance, social consultation and intent to action or drop-off. HIE records where and why drop-off occurs, not just the final answer.

    The sandbox engine is a persistent environment for repeated and counterfactual simulation. Conditions such as price, service availability, policy, weather, rumours or delivery channels can change virtual personas' dynamic states without rewriting the evidence base. The same world can then run scenario A against scenario B, before and after, Monte Carlo runs, sensitivity tests, or a historical replay.

    How virtual personas learn during a run

    Current state›receives a stimulus, question or interaction›interprets it through evidence, priors, trust and context›decides or responds›sees an outcome, social response or new information›updates its dynamic state›next decision

    Virtual personas are persistent in memory within a simulation, but the underlying foundation model is never retrained.

    The engine decides; the language model speaks

    HIE engine determines

    Evidence retrieval, virtual persona state, constraints, behavioral parameters, decision pathway, state transitions, sampling and weighting, and simulation conditions.

    Frozen language model provides

    Situated reasoning, interpretation, natural-language expression and dialogue, using the evidence placed in its context.

    A structured decision policy produces the choice or propensity. The language model then explains it in the virtual persona's voice. It expresses the virtual persona; it does not define it.

    05Confidence at four levels

    HIE scores confidence at four levels, because a well-grounded virtual persona can still give a weak answer on an unfamiliar topic, and a strong answer can still come from a poorly designed study.

    Level 1 · Virtual persona build

    Is this virtual persona sufficiently grounded?

    Evidence coverage, source quality, schema completeness, recency, calibration quality, provenance and how much inference was needed.

    Level 2 · Response

    How trustworthy is this specific answer?

    Retrieval relevance, evidence availability, state consistency, stability across reruns and unsupported inference.

    Level 3 · Panel or study

    How reliable is the aggregated finding?

    Population coverage, sampling-frame fit, weighting, segment representation, method fit, variance across runs and sensitivity to assumptions.

    Level 4 · Virtual World Confidence

    Is this simulation fit for this question?

    Confidence in the quality of the simulation set-up, not the probability that a simulated outcome will occur.

    06Validated against real-world studies

    The clearest test of HIE is backward validation: give the engine only the inputs of a completed real-world study, and compare what its virtual personas say or do with what real people said or did. Across four exercises spanning agriculture, financial inclusion and health, HIE reached 94% to 99.5% benchmark similarity, with a substantially lower sample size and over multiple runs of the same study.

    Backward validation sits alongside five other checks: holdout comparison against real observations not used to build the virtual personas; repeated-run stability across many SLS instantiations; sensitivity testing of assumptions, weights and conditions; expert review of behavioral mechanisms and delivery assumptions; and field calibration, where small real-world samples of original responses are added as new input to recalibrate parameters without replacing the evidence base.

    07What this means for decisions

    HIE lets a team test a policy, program, product, service, or message against thousands of evidence-grounded virtual personas before committing resources, see where and why people would drop off, and compare scenarios side by side. Its outputs are directional hypotheses with an explicit confidence score and a traceable evidence trail. They sharpen where to invest, what to test in the field, and what to ask real people next, rather than replacing them.
