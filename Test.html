The Human
Insights Engine

From real-world evidence to dynamic virtual worlds

How the Human Insights Engine turns evidence into bounded, context-aware, memory-persistent, and auditable virtual personas, and how it checks that what they tell us holds up.

Real world evidence, simulated feedback, informed decisions.

The Human Insights Engine (HIE) is an evidence-informed and AI-powered simulation tool that helps development organisations explore potential responses to a proposed policy, program, product, service or message before resources are committed. It turns relevant real-world evidence into directional hypotheses for a defined population, geography, behavioural question, and use case. It is not a replacement for real-world activities, but a conduit and complementary asset to problem-solve faster, more robustly, and with more resource-efficient approaches. It works very well with established real-world research, data collection, prototyping, pilots, and at-scale implementation practices.

HIE does not write virtual personas as character descriptions or prompts. It converts real-world evidence into machine-readable behavioural agents, places them in structured social and research environments, and runs repeated simulations under explicit rules, constraints and confidence checks. The result is simulation that is bounded by evidence, persistent in memory over time and auditable after the fact.

02How the engine was trained

HIE began as a shell: machine learning code that defines how a virtual persona is constructed, with metrics across four categories: demographics, psychographics, behaviours and context. On its own, the shell knows the shape of a virtual person but nothing about specific characteristics that define it.

We trained it on a wide range of real-world data, including human-centered design primary research, in-field observations, household and national surveys, census data, quantitative research studies, clinical and programme data, longitudinal series data, and more. Large language models (LLMs) made the training fast and feasible, reading and structuring very different sources and mapping them into the shell's common schema. That structured evidence trained the machine learning code and let us find the right weights, turning a static shell into a dynamic machine learning schema that can generate new virtual personas whenever new data is loaded.

The above are a few examples of the types of input data we have used to train the model. The Human Insights Engine doesn't retain the prior training data. The training taught us the quality and quantity of data needed to generate new virtual personas and virtual worlds for a specific problem. That formula is what let us build demo centres, mini virtual worlds dedicated to specific problem-solving spaces.

This has helped us codify the approach needed to build any new set of virtual personas and virtual worlds for specific problem areas, and do it quickly and efficiently. It tells us what kind of data to collect first. Once that foundation is built, it can be reused to solve many more problems, not just the first one.

Two principles that keep the engine honest

Principle 1

Trained only on inputs

When we use a study, we train only on its original responses, never on the study's outputs or findings.

Principle 2

Simulated data never goes back in

Nothing the engine generates is ever fed back into the platform as training data.

Both guard against the same failure. Feeding in simulated data risks hallucination compounding over time. Feeding in a study's outputs risks the model, over time, referencing those answers instead of reasoning from what people actually said and did.

It also means there is only one route to improving HIE: more detailed, more comprehensive, higher-quality input data. When a simulation needs to be more accurate, we improve what goes in, never by feeding back what comes out.
